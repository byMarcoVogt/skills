# Writing

Read before writing or revising text in any medium: chat, documents, letters, slides, page copy. German and English unless an entry says otherwise.

## Language and names

- One language per deliverable, set by its reader. A German page has no anglicism where a plain German word exists ("Ask", "Leadership"). An English posting gets an English application throughout.
- Names of files, folders, skills, columns and statuses are English, short and findable. Proper names and text for a German reader stay German. A figurative name only when the image carries the meaning (approved: "Landkarte", "Prüfstand", rejected: "Fold", "Go-Karten").
- Proper names and titles are written the way their owner writes them: company spelling, a job title exactly as in the ad.
- Internal names stay internal. Text that someone else reads uses the name that reader knows (not agent, file or log names, not "Subagenten" or "Gate"). Exception: viewers who will use a tool get its real technical name.
- German number format: amount, space, then € ("~ 0,5 €"), decimal comma. Notes and sources are numbered (1, 2 or [1]), never lettered, one scheme per document.

## Rations

Allowed only in the case and form named. Everywhere else the check fails.

| Pattern | Allowed when | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Colon inside a sentence | The opening sentence of a post, letter or article sets up a point that follows, a label, or a list announcement | At most one sentence colon per paragraph. Never two colons in one line. Never in personal messages. A colon that only introduces criteria or stages a reveal fails. |
| Announcement before the point | It names a category the reader needs ("Für den Import gilt eine Einschränkung. Dateien über 50 MB werden abgelehnt.") | One sentence, then the content. An announcement that only builds suspense ("Ich habe Neuigkeiten.", "Dann kam die Überraschung:") fails in every register. |
| Contrast "nicht X, sondern Y" / "not X but Y" | It corrects something the reader actually believes | Once per text, in one sentence. As a rhythm, or "nicht nur …, sondern auch", it fails. |
| Series of three | Three different things: objects, methods, steps | Never three words for one thing. In a sentence at most three items. |
| Second "und" in a German sentence | Fixed term pairs ("Forderungen und Verbindlichkeiten") | Otherwise one free "und" per sentence. Split the sentence or use "sowie". |
| Meta lines | A retrieval date ("abgerufen am …"), a source line under a chart or table, one uniform estimate marker ("~") | Anything else about the text (disclaimers, "alte Fassung", date pointers to decisions, "Enter saves") goes. |

## Checklist

Check that the text has:

- no slogan, aphorism or paradox that sounds weighty and says nothing ("Ein System, dessen Schwächen man kennt, ist nicht fehlerfrei. Es ist ungemessen."). A concrete image that says something checkable may stay.
- no higher register than needed: no buzz nouns ("Stakeholder-Management"), officialese ("unquittiert"), academic "Hochdeutsch" or grand English ("a provisional account") where a plainer word is as precise. A term that names a specific thing stays.
- no title, callout or caption that is clever where a plain descriptive sentence fits. Numbers as the result ("die 16 fehlenden"), not as arithmetic.
- no claim stronger than its evidence, and no double hedge ("könnten möglicherweise").
- no synonyms for one concept within a text, and no term the user replaced.
- no word or verb repeated within one block or between a heading and its box.
- no spin, no tacked-on admission ("…; das war mein Fehler"), no overclaim and no self-belittling in text about the user. A weakness is said plainly in one calm sentence.
- first person where the user is the actor ("ich", the user's name), not "der Mensch", passive or system-state phrasing.
- no growth: a revision is not longer than the text it replaces unless content was added, and a "shortened" text is visibly shorter.
- no fixed set of labelled lines repeated for every item when a label carries nothing for that item.
- the form follows the content: an argument as prose, rules and options as labelled bullets with a lead sentence. Neither a wall of paragraphs nor a flat bullet dump.
- no Markdown syntax leaking into a target that does not render it (Word, Docs, email, portals).
